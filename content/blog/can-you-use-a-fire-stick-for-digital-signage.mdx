**Short answer: only for a quick test or a screen you can restart by hand.** A regular Fire TV Stick on FireOS 8 or later can no longer open a signage app by itself when the TV powers on. After every power cut or TV restart, someone has to pick up the remote and launch the app. For a screen that should run unattended, that is a deal-breaker.

Amazon sells a separate device for this job, the **Signage Stick**, which boots straight into your signage software.

## What changed: FireOS 8 removed auto-start

For years the Fire Stick was the cheap default for small signage setups. It cost around $40, plugged into any HDMI port, and several signage apps ran on it. Mvix, for example, launched a Fire TV signage app back in 2016.

Then Amazon changed the operating system. Starting with **FireOS 8 (February 2024)**, apps can no longer start automatically when the device boots. OptiSigns documents the effect in its [support guide](https://support.optisigns.com/hc/en-us/articles/1500009042742-FireOS-TVs-Amazon-Insigna-Toshiba-TCL): on FireOS 7 and earlier, its app opens when the TV turns on. On FireOS 8 and later, the app shows a message that the device does not support autostart, and someone has to open it with the remote after every restart. OptiSigns now states that it no longer recommends Fire OS TVs or Fire TV Sticks as digital signage players.

Why it matters for a real screen:

- A shop opens, the power flickers overnight, and the screen shows the Fire TV home screen instead of your offers.
- A restaurant menu board goes blank after a power cut and stays that way until a staff member notices.
- Multi-screen setups need someone to restart every stick by hand.

There is an ADB-based workaround (a command-line tool for Android devices), but OptiSigns describes it as inconvenient, and it is not something most small business owners will want to maintain.

## The device Amazon built for signage: the Signage Stick

In late 2024 Amazon launched the **Amazon Signage Stick**, a separate product from the regular Fire TV Stick. Based on the vendor pages we checked:

| | Regular Fire TV Stick | Amazon Signage Stick |
|---|---|---|
| Auto-starts your signage app on power-on | No (FireOS 8 and later) | Yes (kiosk-style boot into your signage software) |
| Price at launch | About $59.99 (4K Max list price) | $99.99 (later seen on sale for $84.99) |
| Built for 24/7 signage | No | Yes |
| Includes signage software | No | No, you still need a signage platform subscription |
| 4K output | Model dependent | Yes |
| Portrait and landscape | Depends on the app | Supported |
| Remote management | Depends on the app | Screenshot capture, restart and device info through a compatible platform |

Two limits to know about:

- **It is not signage software.** The stick plays whatever your signage platform sends it. You still need a compatible platform subscription.
- **Display on/off depends on your TV.** Switching the screen on and off remotely relies on HDMI-CEC 2.0, and support varies between TV brands.

## So can you use a regular Fire Stick for signage?

It depends on how the screen is used.

**A regular Fire Stick is fine if:**

- You are testing a signage platform before buying hardware.
- The screen is staffed and someone can relaunch the app after every restart.
- It is a one-off event or a temporary display.
- The Fire Stick is on FireOS 7 or earlier and you never update it. This will not last, since updates can arrive automatically.

**Do not use a regular Fire Stick if:**

- The screen needs to run unattended for days or weeks.
- You have several locations and cannot visit each one.
- The screen is customer facing and a blank or home screen looks unprofessional.
- You rely on Wi-Fi only. The Fire TV Stick 4K Max has no Ethernet port, so a weak signal means a stalled screen.

## What to buy instead

Pick based on how many screens you run and how much hands-on work you can accept.

| Option | Best for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| **Amazon Signage Stick** | One to a few screens, small business budget | Needs a signage platform subscription. Display on/off relies on HDMI-CEC. |
| **Raspberry Pi** | Low cost, tinkerers, managed setups | Reviewers report SD card failures, especially after power cuts. Use quality cards and a stable power supply. |
| **Android signage box** | Mid-range reliability, multiple screens | Consumer Android TV boxes are fine at home but limited for large deployments. Look for devices sold for signage. |
| **Mini PC or Chromebox** | Heavy content, video walls, web apps | Higher price and more setup |
| **BrightSign** | Large, long-term, mission-critical networks | Higher price. Check current pricing, since older lines have been discontinued. |
| **Screen with a built-in player** | Cleaner installs with fewer cables | Tied to the display brand. Check which software it supports. |

## Checklist before you buy any player

1. Which signage software will you use? Confirm it officially supports the player.
2. Does the player start your app automatically after a power cut?
3. Can you manage and restart it remotely?
4. Does it have Ethernet, or are you depending on Wi-Fi?
5. Does it support your content (video, 4K, portrait mode)?
6. How many screens will you add in the next year?
7. What is the total cost, including the software subscription?

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I use a Fire Stick as a digital signage player?**
Yes for testing or staffed screens. For unattended signage, no, because FireOS 8 and later does not let apps start automatically after a restart.

**What is the Amazon Signage Stick?**
A Fire TV style stick made for signage. It boots into your signage software on power-on, supports 4K, and can be managed remotely through a compatible platform. It does not include signage software.

**Does a Fire Stick start my signage app after a power cut?**
Not on FireOS 8 or later. On FireOS 7 and earlier, apps such as OptiSigns can autostart, but you should not plan a new setup around an old OS version.

**Is a Fire Stick cheaper than a Raspberry Pi or Android signage box?**
The regular stick costs less up front, but the cost of someone restarting it by hand, or a blank screen, can outweigh the saving.

**Do I still need a subscription with the Signage Stick?**
Yes. The stick is only the player. You need a signage platform to manage content.

**What is the most reliable digital signage player?**
It depends on scale. BrightSign is the traditional choice for large networks. For small setups, the Signage Stick, a good Android signage box, or a well-managed Raspberry Pi are common options.
